Coffee Futures Market Weekly Update

The Sep 28 report flagged a bounce off the 3-month low. Across the three weeks since (Sep 28–Oct 16), that bounce has grown into an attempted trend reversal. December arabica (KCZ26) jumped +10.75¢ (+3.65%) on Friday to settle at 305.55¢ (intraday high 311.20¢), up +18.60¢ (+6.48%) on the week and +26.95¢ (+9.67%) from the last report’s 278.60¢ close. The catalysts were ICE certified arabica stocks falling again (238,976 bags on Oct 15), dealer warnings that Brazilian coffee may not reach ICE warehouses before the December contract expires, and El Niño worries for the 2027 crop. London robusta did not follow, slipping 1.6% over the same week, so this rally looks like an arabica-specific event driven by deliverable supply and quality.

Weekly Market Summary

The Sep 28 report said the market’s centre of gravity was shifting from today’s supply to the 2027 crop, and that the certified-stock rebuild had stalled on grading. The following three weeks of data point the same way on both counts. Certified stocks fell to a 27-year low of 217,646 bags on Sep 15, rebounded to 260,654 bags on Sep 30, then dropped again to 238,976 bags on Oct 15 (-2,971 bags that day). That is -6.0% versus the ~254,300 bags of Sep 24. The rebuild did not hold this cycle either.

Price broke out of the top of its range. December arabica spiked to 304.45¢ (+4.07%) on Oct 6, a 3.5-week high, reversed -3.86% the next day, and slid to 286.95¢ by Oct 9 (a two-week low intraday). This week it added +11.10¢ Monday through Wednesday, paused with -3.25¢ on Thursday, and surged +10.75¢ Friday to close at 305.55¢. That level is above the 270–285¢ box of the last report and above the Sep 5 report base (297.45¢).

What matters is the nature of the rally. The bearish narrative — Brazil’s record crop (Conab 67.6m bags) and the ICO’s first surplus in five years — is unchanged, yet price rose. The market is pricing December deliverable supply and 2027 weather, not total supply. London robusta fell from $3,470 to $3,414 (-1.6%) on Vietnam’s advancing harvest, so this is a shortage of deliverable arabica rather than a broad improvement in demand.

Key dynamic this week: The last report said “not all arriving coffee is exchange grade.” This week it is “it may not arrive before December expiry.” Finimize (Oct 16) reported dealers warning that Brazilian beans may reach ICE warehouses only after the December contract expires, flagging thin deliverable supply and a possible nearby premium. Safras’ Barabach said the effect so far shows up mainly in spreads between coffee qualities rather than a structural shift in the price curve. The key dates are Nov 12 (options expiry) and Nov 19 (first notice day).

Certified stocks rebounded from 217,646 bags (a 27-year low) in mid-September to 260,654 bags on Sep 30, then fell back to 238,976 bags. The previous report noted a high rejection rate on Brazilian arrivals. The specific cause of this decline (withdrawals, grading failures) and the grading pass/fail numbers were not confirmed in this research.

Stocks are under 40% of the year-ago level (~643k bags, cited in the previous report). The thinner the exchange-grade supply available for December delivery, the more the nearby contract earns a premium over deferred months and the more small logistics news moves price. Friday’s 12.70¢ intraday range shows this.

Implication: The market is pricing not “will Brazilian coffee come?” but “will it come on time and pass grading?” Unless certified stocks rise meaningfully before the Nov 19 first notice day, nearby premium and volatility should persist. If stocks recover to the 260k range, much of this rally could unwind. For buyers, December roll cost and securing exchange-grade lots now matter as much as the price level.

Despite higher prices, the supply outlook itself has not changed. Conab raised Brazil’s 2026 crop to 67.6m bags (Sep 24; arabica +34.8%, robusta -6.6%). The ICO (Sep 10) put 2025/26 world production at a record 183.6m bags (+4.4%) against consumption of 180.6m (-0.9%), a 3m-bag surplus, the first in five years. USDA (Jul 22) sees 2026/27 world output up 6.0% to 189.7m bags (arabica +12%). Among private houses, Hedgepoint estimates a global surplus of 8.9m bags (via Trading Economics) and StoneX about 10m bags for 2026 (cited in the previous report).

Implication: The 2026 “volume” bearish narrative remains valid but is priced in (price is still about 9% below the 335.50¢ August high). Price-setting power has moved to near-term deliverable supply and 2027 weather. A long-term surplus coexisting with near-term tightness shows up as curve structure (possible backwardation) and higher volatility.

3. Weather — Normal Rain, but El Niño Is the Wild Card

Somar recorded 33.3mm in Minas Gerais in the week ended Oct 4 (104% of average) — normal rainfall. The September excess-rain phase (242% of average) is over and moisture for flowering appears maintained. Commercial warns that El Niño could delay Brazil’s Sept–Oct rains, and the US CPC says this event could be among the strongest in 75 years. A USDA report relayed by ADMIS this week also said El Niño would likely hurt Brazilian output. Somar rainfall figures after the week of Oct 4 could not be confirmed and are excluded.

Implication: A 2027 off-year in the biennial cycle combined with El Niño raises downside risk to the 2027/28 supply outlook. With rainfall at normal levels, this is a “concern priced in” stage, not “damage confirmed.” The distribution of October–November rain is the biggest directional input for price.

Cecafé data (Revista Cultivar, Oct 9) put September exports at 3.894m bags. On a Jan–Sep basis arabica is still -10.7% y/y while conilon is +68.3%, so this year’s export growth is robusta-led. Brazil’s arabica bumper crop may not be fully converting into exports, or may be moving through non-exchange channels because of quality issues. That reading fits Safras’ comments on low-grade beans and possible crop downgrades, but it is not direct proof of causation.

5. Robusta — Left Out of the Rally

London November robusta rose to $3,590 on Oct 6 (a 6-week high) before closing $3,470 on Oct 9 and $3,414 (+1.16%) on Oct 16 — -$56 (-1.61%) on the week, diverging from arabica (+6.48%). Safras said robusta did not follow arabica’s rally because of Vietnam’s advancing harvest and expectations of greater supply ahead. Vietnam’s September exports were +53% and Jan–Sep +16.2%.

The New York–London spread (arabica 305.55¢ minus robusta ~154.9¢/lb) is about 150.7¢/lb, up sharply from roughly 126¢ in the last report (derived conversion) and very wide by historical standards.

Blending implication: With the arabica-robusta gap above 150¢/lb, the cost saving from raising robusta share in blends has grown. After Vietnam’s harvest peaks in November, robusta may also become more volatile.

6. Positioning and Technical Structure

CFTC data (as of Oct 13, released Oct 16) show ICE Coffee C managed money long at 35,096 (-1,947) and short at 19,826 (+1,819), for a net long of +15,270. That is -3,766 from the prior week (long 37,043 / short 18,007, net +19,036). The data are as of Tuesday and do not capture the Wednesday–Friday surge, so they confirm only that funds did not lead the rally. Open interest was 163,626 contracts. This COT figure is single-source (CFTC primary data).

On the chart, December climbed in steps from 286.95¢ (10/9) to an intraday 303.35¢ on 10/14 and 311.20¢ on 10/16. Observed price levels: resistance at 311.20¢ (10/16 high) and 308.35¢ (10/7 high); support at 298.50¢ (10/16 low), 290.60¢ (10/12 close) and 283.50¢ (10/12 intraday low).

7. FX — A Stronger Real Curbs Producer Selling

USD/BRL fell from about 5.19 on 9/25 to 4.99 on 10/9, then traded near 5.10 on 10/16 (PoundSterlingLive, indicative rates). Safras reported the dollar fell below R$5.00 after the first-round election, and Barchart said the real hit a 4.75-month high on Oct 6. A stronger real reduces Brazilian producers’ incentive to sell dollars, supporting price. December in reais works out to about R$2,060/bag (305.55¢ × 132.276 × 5.10), roughly +7.7% from the previous report’s R$1,913 (derived estimate).

Implication: Higher dollar prices and a stronger real together lifted producers’ real-denominated receipts even more. We found no evidence of a major jump in selling, but if Brazilian growers sell into higher prices, it could cap the rally.

Implications

1. This rally is a deliverable-supply shortage, not a reversal in total supply. The surplus forecasts (ICO 3m bags, StoneX 10m bags) stand and robusta fell. Price could therefore swing sharply around December expiry and the Nov 19 first notice day, which may reflect expiry-event volatility as much as a lasting trend change.

2. The wider arabica-robusta gap creates structural substitution pressure. The spread has widened to about 150¢/lb (derived). Roasters able to adjust blends have a stronger incentive to raise robusta share, which over the medium term caps the upside for arabica.

3. Funds have not yet followed the rally. Managed-money net long fell as of Oct 13. If the next COT (released Oct 23) shows a large increase, it signals chasing; if it falls, the rally is commercial and hedge-driven. Light positioning means both further upside room and liquidation pressure on a drop are relatively limited.

4. Buyers: manage expiry and quality rather than chase highs. December broke above the last report’s 270–285¢ box. Rather than fix everything at once, combine staged fixing with options, and check roll and delivery timing around Nov 12–19 and the availability of exchange-grade lots. Keep spec and sample-approval discipline given concerns over low-grade Brazilian lots.

5. The 2027 El Niño risk is still at the “concern” stage. Rainfall is near normal (104%) and there is no confirmed damage data. Once the distribution of the next 4–6 weeks of rain is known, a sizable reassessment in either direction could follow.

Market Outlook & Risks

Short-Term Structure (1–3 Months)

In the near term, December is likely to probe direction in a 290–312¢ zone. Initial boundaries are 311.20¢ and 308.35¢ on the upside and 298.50¢ and 290.60¢ on the downside. The variables that will decide direction are, in order: ① ICE certified stock trend, ② Nov 12 options expiry and Nov 19 first notice day, ③ October–November Brazilian rain and El Niño, ④ the real, and ⑤ the next COT.

Note: the probabilities below are the analyst’s subjective judgment on market flow, not statistical forecasts.
Base case (~45%): high-volatility 290–312¢ range. Stocks edge up or stall, El Niño fears clash with surplus forecasts, and price oscillates into expiry.
Upside case (~30%): break above 312¢. Certified stocks fall further toward 220k bags, Brazilian arrivals slip, and nearby premium widens. A dry October–November would accelerate the move.
Downside case (~25%): back below 290¢. Brazilian arrivals and grading passes rise, stocks recover to the 260k range, rainfall normalizes and the real weakens.

Medium-Term Outlook (3–12 Months)

The ICO, USDA, Hedgepoint and StoneX all point to surplus, so we see gentle downward pressure on the medium-term trend. The 2027 variables — a biennial off-year and a strong El Niño — add volatility to that downward path. Until certified stocks normalize, the near-term curve can stay tight.

Key Risk Factors

  • Upside — deliverable shortfall: If Brazilian coffee does not arrive before December expiry, nearby premium and volatility widen.
  • Upside — El Niño: Confirmed delay in Sept–Oct rains would lower the 2027/28 outlook.
  • Upside — origin supply disruptions: Colombia output -15%, Uganda exports -13.7% and Central American drought constrain washed arabica.
  • Downside — structural surplus: Conab 67.6m bags, ICO 3m-bag surplus, USDA record output forecast.
  • Downside — robusta substitution: Vietnam’s harvest progress and a 150¢/lb gap encourage blend shifts.
  • Downside — producer selling: Brazilian growers may sell more at 305¢+.
  • Downside — rally unwind: A rise that funds have not joined can reverse quickly if stocks improve.

Overall Assessment

The core change of the past three weeks is that the pricing benchmark has shifted from “2026 total supply” to “December deliverable supply and 2027 weather.” December rose to 305.55¢, +9.67% from the last report and +6.48% on the week, yet robusta fell and fund net length shrank over the same period. This rally is therefore not broad demand improvement or speculative buying but a price created by scarcity of exchange-grade arabica.

The medium-term surplus picture (ICO 3m bags, StoneX 10m bags, USDA record output) has not moved. Our base view is a high-volatility 290–312¢ range, with the Nov 12 options expiry and Nov 19 first notice day potentially marking peak short-term volatility. For procurement, a combination of staged fixing, option hedges and tight roll and quality-spec management is more sensible than chasing the top of the range.
