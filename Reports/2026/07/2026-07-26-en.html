Weekly Market Summary

This week's arc was "a second straight decline, then a Friday rebound." Arabica September slipped through midweek to a 2.5-week low of 309.4¢ on Thursday (7/23) (-7.25¢/-2.29%), with December falling below 300¢ to 296.45¢. On Friday (7/24), pre-weekend short-covering lifted the market +4.40¢ (+1.42%) to ~313.8¢. Even so, the week closed -6.90¢ (a second consecutive fall), and NY weekly volume was the lowest since the week of May 26 — thin liquidity throughout.

The real driver was supply. Last week's harvest-delay rebound reversed into harvest-acceleration expectations amid dry weather, while new-crop flows from Brazil and Indonesia weighed on robusta. A record Brazil crop and surplus consensus (StoneX Brazil 75.3M, global surplus ~10M) was reaffirmed, and Cecafé's strong June exports (+14.4% y/y) underscored the volume flow.

The biggest narrative shift was the resolution of tariff uncertainty. Last week's top wildcard — the US 25% Brazil tariff (effective 7/22) — was confirmed to exempt green coffee, and instant coffee was also exempted (protecting $2.0–2.5B in annual exports). With the supply-disruption premium removed, prices reverted to pure supply-demand logic. The floor was held by ICE stocks at a ~2-year low and a firm real.

The week's core dynamic: If last week was a "harvest-delay rebound," this week was a "renewed decline on harvest acceleration, new-crop supply, and confirmed tariff exemptions." As near-term downside (harvest pace, record crop, new-crop exports) fought the floor (low stocks, firm real), the upside catalyst (tariffs) faded, strengthening the medium-term bearish case. Friday's bounce looks like short-covering in thin liquidity.

Fundamental Analysis

1. Production — record crop and surplus consensus reaffirmed

The medium-term price is defined by a record Brazil crop. USDA FAS sees Brazil 2026/27 at a record 71.9M bags (+14% y/y), with arabica at 47.5M (+25%). This week StoneX raised Brazil to 75.3M bags and sees a global surplus of ~10M bags; Rabobank pegs the surplus at 7–10M (arabica surplus 9.5M). Institutional arabica targets sit at $2.50–3.00.

Implication: The consensus that the 2021–2024 deficit cycle has given way to a structural surplus cycle was reaffirmed this week. But the surplus is still a "forecast"; until it is confirmed in warehouse stocks, low inventories and weather can keep triggering counter-trend rallies.

2. Weather & Harvest — acceleration drives near-term downside

The real driver of this week's decline was harvest-acceleration expectations. Brazil's harvest was 64% complete as of July 15, behind last year's 77% and the 5-year average of 70%, but per Somar Meteorologia, Minas Gerais rainfall was just 0.2mm (20% of normal) in the week to July 19 — pointing to a faster harvest pace that capped the top. Against this, the US Climate Prediction Center's warning of an El Niño "strongest in 75 years" keeps an asymmetric upside risk around Sep–Oct flowering (the 27/28 crop) and winter frost.

3. Exports — Brazil, Vietnam, Indonesia new crop support the downside

Supply keeps flowing. Brazil's June green-coffee exports rebounded to 2.64M bags (+14.4% y/y), and Vietnam's H1 exports were firm at 1.1M tonnes (+9.7% y/y). Indonesia's robusta (Lampung) new crop is wrapping up, lifting near-term shipments and pressuring robusta. Yet Vietnam's average export price fell 22%, underscoring a "plenty of volume, falling prices" new-crop structure.

5. Macro & Positioning — firm real, index roll ahead

USD/BRL: the real sat at 5.085 on 7/24 (-0.29%), near a 7-week high (weekly range 5.06–5.12; ~+2.1% on the month). High Selic and carry demand support the real, which weakens Brazilian producer selling incentive — a support for coffee. CFTC positioning (as of 7/14): Coffee C managed-money net long ~32,834 contracts (down from 34,454 on 7/7), with long liquidation under way. Alongside the fresh COT (as of 7/21) released Friday, the index-fund roll beginning August 7 means heavy September length in both markets could amplify downside volatility over the next 3–4 weeks.

Implications

1. Tariff wildcard removed → a pure supply-demand phase: Last week's top variable is settled — green and instant coffee are exempt. With the supply-disruption premium gone, prices again respond to pure fundamentals (harvest pace, stocks, new-crop exports). For roasters and traders, this means the risk of a spike in US sourcing costs has eased.

2. Surplus is a forecast; low stocks are reality: The record crop and surplus (Brazil 71.9–75.3M, global 10M) remain a "forecast," while ICE stocks at a ~2-year low (~315.6K) and just 3.3% Brazil-origin are "reality." Until that gap closes via warehouse accumulation, downside speed is limited and counter-trend/short-covering bounces can recur.

3. Not a crash — a grind lower: As Friday's bounce showed, thin liquidity, low stocks, and a firm real restrain any one-way plunge. The trend points down, but likely gradually. Thin volume raises the risk of two-way gaps.

4. El Niño / frost = residual asymmetric upside: The CPC's "strongest in 75 years" warning raises Sep–Oct flowering (27/28) and winter-frost risk. Against a bumper-crop, surplus consensus, low-cost call-option hedges remain cost-effective.

Market Outlook & Risks

Near-term structure (1–3 months)

The trend is a gradual grind lower with shallow bounces. Support runs 300¢ (psychological) then 296¢ (December low); resistance 320¢ then 330¢. Harvest acceleration, the record crop, and new-crop exports cap the top, while low stocks and a firm real hold the floor — a gently downward-tilting range. Position unwinding ahead of the August 7 index roll is the first inflection point.

Technical view: A 2.5-week low of 309.4¢ Thursday, then a 313.8¢ Friday rebound. December is sub-300 (296.45¢). Resistance 320/330¢, support 300/296¢. In thin volume, frost/weather headlines can gap the tape. Watch downside volatility if heavy September length in robusta and arabica unwinds into the index roll (from 8/7).

Medium-term outlook (3–12 months)

As Brazil's harvest advances and new-crop supply ramps, the record surplus from USDA (71.9M), StoneX (Brazil 75.3M, surplus 10M), and Rabobank (surplus 7–10M) is likely to be confirmed in warehouses, favoring medium-term downside. But ① ~2-year-low stocks, ② El Niño (strongest in 75 years) 27/28 flowering risk, and ③ positioning rolls act as buffers/disruptors, making "structural drift lower plus intermittent weather-driven spikes" the base case. Institutional arabica targets sit at $2.50–3.00.

Key risk factors

  • Structural supply (downside): Brazil 71.9–75.3M, global surplus 10M, Vietnam +9.7%, Indonesia new crop — a medium-term bearish anchor.
  • Brazil weather (two-way): Continued dryness accelerates harvest (down); returning rain delays it and hurts quality (up).
  • El Niño (27/28, upside): CPC "strongest in 75 years" — asymmetric upside if Sep–Oct flowering rains are disrupted.
  • ICE stocks (floor cushion): ~2.25-year low (~315.6K, 3.3% Brazil-origin) restrains sharp drops.
  • Positioning (downside): Heavy September length — roll/unwind risk into the 8/7 index roll.
  • Tariffs (resolved, residual): Green/instant exemptions removed the upside catalyst; policy-change risk remains a residual variable.

Overall Analysis

This week crossed a second straight decline with a Friday short-covering rebound. September arabica hit a 2.5-week low of 309.4¢ Thursday before bouncing +4.40¢ to ~313.8¢ Friday, yet closed -6.90¢ on the week. Near-term downside (harvest acceleration, record crop, new-crop exports) is fighting a floor cushion (low stocks, firm real).

The most important change is the disappearance of the tariff upside catalyst. With green and instant coffee exempted, US sourcing-spike risk has eased and the market has reverted to pure supply-demand logic. As long as the forecast-vs-reality gap persists — surplus still a forecast, stocks still at a ~2-year low — the downside is more likely a gradual grind with short-covering bounces than a crash.

Trading takeaway: A near-term 296–330¢ box, with 300/296¢ support and 320/330¢ resistance as the pivots. Key monitors: ① Brazil harvest pace and rainfall ② ICE stocks and pending grading ③ the fresh COT (7/21) and the 8/7 index roll ④ robusta new crop (Indonesia/Vietnam) ⑤ El Niño CPC updates ⑥ any tariff-policy change.

For roasters and importers, scaling into the down-move to manage average cost — while hedging the top with low-cost calls against an El Niño/frost re-spike — is the sensible parallel strategy. Until the structural surplus is confirmed in warehouse stocks, one-way bets warrant caution.
