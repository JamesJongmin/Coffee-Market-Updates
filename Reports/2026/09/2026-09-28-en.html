Coffee Futures Market Weekly Update

Coffee Futures Market Weekly Update

If last week’s story was the start of the certified-stock rebuild, this week’s story is that the rebuild stalled. ICE certified arabica stocks slipped from 258,415 bags on Sep 18 to roughly 254,300 bags on Sep 24, and Tuesday’s grading saw 5,212 bags of Brazilian coffee rejected against 4,771 approved. December arabica (KCZ26) touched a 3-month intraday low of 269.75¢ on Wednesday as Conab raised Brazil’s crop to a record 67.6 million bags, then rallied on dollar-driven short covering to settle Friday at 278.60¢. That is -1.90¢ (-0.68%) on the week — a fourth straight weekly loss, but a much smaller one. Meanwhile INMET issued a red heat-wave alert (Sep 26–30) covering South Minas flowering areas, shifting the market’s centre of gravity from today’s supply to the 2027 crop.

Weekly Market Summary

Last week’s report argued that the rise in certified stocks was “likely a multi-week flow rather than a one-off,” citing roughly 49,000 bags awaiting grading. This week validated that thesis only halfway. The pending queue was indeed worked through (about 49,000 bags on Sep 16 → about 18,000 on Sep 23), but much of it failed to make it into certified stock. In Tuesday’s grading alone, 4,771 bags passed and 5,212 bags of Brazilian coffee were rejected — more than half of what was presented. Certified stocks therefore went sideways: 258,415 (Sep 18) → 253,355 (Mon) → 256,678 (Tue) → 254,480 (Wed) → ~254,300 (Thu).

Prices digested cross-currents. Monday fell -4.10¢ (Somar rainfall 242% of normal, surging Cecáfé shipments) and Tuesday another -4.10¢ to 272.30¢. On Wednesday December hit 269.75¢ intraday, a roughly 3-month low, leaving the drawdown from the Aug 25 close of 335.50¢ at -19.6%, just shy of -20%. But Wednesday closed +3.60¢ (+1.3%) higher as oversold conditions triggered fund short covering. Thursday lost only -0.55¢ despite Conab’s record crop estimate, and on Friday a weaker dollar index sparked more short covering for a +3.25¢ gain to a 1-week high.

The essence of the week is “a market that falls less on bad news.” A record Brazil crop (Conab 67.6m bags), September shipments running ~39% ahead of the prior month and robusta certified stocks at a 10-month high all landed in one week — yet December’s weekly loss was only -0.68%, about a third of last week’s -1.82%, and the close-to-close range halved from 14.00¢ to 6.30¢. After losing more than 60¢ in a month, price appears to have discounted much of the supply news.

Core dynamic of the week: Last week the market reacted to ‘the coffee has arrived.’ This week it learned that ‘not all of the coffee that arrived is exchange grade.’ The gap between Brazil’s total supply (Conab 67.6m bags) and deliverable exchange supply (certified stocks in the 250k-bag range) became starker than at any point this season. Procafé’s reports of rising Fusarium concolor incidence and a larger share of ground-swept (varrição) coffee explain the gap — a record crop, but the most uneven quality in years.

Key News Briefing

  • (9/21 Mon, Somar Meteorologia) Minas Gerais received 33.4 mm of rain, 242% of normal, in the week to Sep 20. Down from 59.4 mm (1,212%) the prior week but still well above average, and seen as favourable for flowering and flower set (pegamento).
  • (9/21 Mon, Reuters/CocoaIntel) After an unusually wet winter, the first major flowering for the 2027 crop came about a month early across several Brazilian regions. Agronomists said that, if development is normal, part of the 2027 arabica harvest could start around May. 2027 remains an off-year in the biennial cycle.
  • (9/22 Tue, ICE) Arabica grading: 4,771 bags approved, 5,212 Brazilian bags rejected. Certified stocks 256,678 bags, 20,157 bags pending. Versus the prior Wednesday (16,210 passed, 2,960 rejected), the rejection rate jumped from ~15% to over 50%.
  • (9/22 Tue, Cecáfé) Brazil shipped 2,304,002 bags through Sep 22 (arabica 1,687,181; conilon 452,374; soluble 164,447), ~39% above the comparable prior-month period, averaging 104,727 bags/day. Certificates of origin reached 2.974m bags, leaving a ~670k-bag pipeline above shipments.
  • (9/22 Tue, ABIC/Reuters) Brazil’s domestic consumption reached 13.69m bags in Jan–Aug, +2.29% (+4.65% in May–Aug). Retail prices fell y/y by 21.22% for Traditional/Extra Strong and 19.31% for soluble, reversing part of 2025’s price-driven demand contraction (-5.4%).
  • (9/23 Wed, ICE/Reuters) December hit 269.75¢ (~3-month low) before reversing to settle at 275.90¢ (+3.60¢). Certified stocks 254,480 bags (643,341 a year earlier), with only ~18,000 bags pending. London robusta touched $3,218 (a 3.5-month low) before recovering to $3,317.
  • (9/24 Thu, Conab 3rd survey) Brazil’s 2026 crop raised to 67.6m bags (+19.6%; from 66.7m) — a record. Arabica 48.21m bags (+34.8%), robusta/conilon 19.39m (-6.6%). Area 1.95m ha (+5.1%), yield 34.6 bags/ha (+13.8%). Espírito Santo conilon fell to 12.1m bags (-14.3%) after cold damage at flowering.
  • (9/24 Thu, Procafé/market commentary) Higher Fusarium incidence this harvest has increased reddish-discoloured and Rio/Riado beans. Unofficial talk of at least 10% arabica losses and ~35% ground-swept coffee remains unverified. The price gap between poor ground-swept coffee and good lots exceeds R$1,000/bag; good South Minas coffee was indicated near R$1,700/bag.
  • (9/24 Thu, ICE/Barchart) ICE robusta certified stocks rose to 5,398 lots, a 10-month high. StoneX calculated a 3,020-tonne increase in robusta certified stocks over Sep 1–23.
  • (9/25 Fri, INMET) Red (highest-level) heat-wave alert for Sep 26–30 across 190 municipalities in Minas Gerais — including core South Minas coffee towns Varginha, Lavras, Poços de Caldas and Passos — with temperatures up to 5°C above average.
  • (9/25 Fri, StoneX/Perfect Daily Grind) StoneX Specialty Coffee expects the world market to swing to a 10m-bag surplus in 2026 (1.8m in 2025). The ICO has projected a 3.0m-bag surplus for 2025/26, the first in five years.

Fundamental Analysis

1. Stocks — The Rebuild Is Blocked by Grade, Not Volume

Last week certified stocks jumped 13.9% in three sessions and broke the ‘27-year-low’ narrative. This week the trend stopped: from 258,415 bags on Sep 18 (a 1.5-month high) to 253,355 on Monday (-5,060), 256,678 Tuesday, 254,480 Wednesday and ~254,300 Thursday (-176). The key point is that stocks did not grow even as the queue shrank. Nearly 30,000 pending bags were processed, yet net certified additions were essentially zero. A large part of the queue failed grading.

This fits Procafé’s quality findings exactly. Brazil’s harvest-season winter was unusually wet and picking was delayed, so Fusarium spread through fruit left too long on trees and on the ground. Discoloured and Rio/Riado beans struggle to meet ICE certification standards (defect count and cup). Last week’s Expocacer data — ~30% ground-swept coffee and ~21% sorting rate in the Cerrado — points the same way. In short, ‘Brazilian coffee is coming to the exchange’ is true; ‘enough exchange-grade coffee is coming’ is not yet proven.

Implication: At ~254k bags, certified stocks are only about 40% of a year ago (643,341). If the rebuild is bottlenecked by grade, December’s nearby premium (backwardation) may persist longer than expected. Indeed, Dec/Mar narrowed from 8.20¢ last week to 7.60¢ on Tuesday but stabilised at 7.65¢ Thursday, while Dec/May actually firmed from 10.05¢ to 10.20¢. Where last week we wrote that “the value of paying up for prompt delivery fell sharply,” the revised call is “flattening continues, but at a slower pace.” Buyers should prepare for a widening price gap between exchange-grade (good) and off-grade lots.

Conab’s third survey, taken with 99% of area harvested, is effectively final. The 67.6m-bag total is the largest in Conab’s series, and the increase is entirely arabica: 48.21m bags (+34.8%), about 71% of the total. Minas Gerais arabica alone is 34.96m bags (15.93m in the south and centre-west). Robusta fell 6.6% as Espírito Santo conilon dropped to 12.1m bags (-14.3%) after cold at flowering; Rondônia (2.88m, +24%) only partly offset.

Conab remains conservative relative to other agencies: USDA FAS has 71.9m bags and StoneX about 75.3m. Having already priced in 70m+ bags, the market treated Conab’s upgrade as ‘confirmation, not news’ — hence only -0.20% on Thursday.

Implication: Uncertainty over the quantity of Brazil’s 2026 supply is now essentially resolved. What remains is (1) the share that converts into export- and exchange-grade coffee (quality) and (2) the 2027 crop (weather). Both produced new signals this week — quality via grading rejections, weather via the heat alert. It is reasonable to conclude that the 2026 crop has largely exhausted its value as a bearish driver.

3. Weather — The First Heat Test After the Rains

Flowering-season weather clearly moved into a second phase this week. Phase one (early-to-mid September) was ‘too much rain’: Somar had Minas at 59.4 mm (1,212%) in the week to Sep 13 and 33.4 mm (242%) to Sep 20, and the wet winter triggered a first flowering a month early. Phase two is ‘then comes the heat.’ INMET issued a red heat-wave alert for Sep 26–30 that includes core South Minas towns such as Varginha, Lavras and Poços de Caldas. Brazilian authorities put the probability of a ‘very strong El Niño’ in Sep–Nov above 90%, and INMET’s spring outlook expects above-normal temperatures in Oct–Dec.

Agronomically, the most dangerous sequence is ‘rain that triggers flowering → followed by heat and dryness.’ Heat right after bloom raises evapotranspiration and can cut fruit set and scorch flowers (estrelinha). For balance, the official INMET-INPE outlook also sees above-normal Oct–Dec rainfall in centre-south Minas. The risk is not total rainfall but distribution plus temperature: northern and central Minas face greater moisture stress, while South Minas could suffer if ample rain arrives irregularly.

Implication: Field reports on the Sep 26–30 heat will arrive in the first two weeks of October. Timing is poor: the early (early/mid-September) bloom is already moving into fruit set. Add last week’s hail (at least 10 municipalities in South Minas and the Cerrado) and disease pressure from persistent humidity. With 2027 an off-year, the capacity to absorb losses is small. The market currently attaches almost no premium to 2027 risk — last week’s view that “2027 upside optionality has never been cheaper” is reinforced by this week’s heat alert.

Cecáfé’s September shipments climbed steeply: ~1.45m bags (Sep 16) → 1.82m (Sep 18) → 2.05m (Sep 21, +28.2%) → 2.30m (Sep 22). With certificates of origin at 2.97m bags, full-month September shipments look set to far exceed last year. MDIC’s August exports were also a record 3.8m bags for the month (+43.2%). Still, Jan–Aug cumulative exports of 24.6m bags remain 6.6% below last year — the first-half shortfall is not yet fully recovered.

Exports vs certified stocks: Shipments are booming while certified stocks stall. Read together, the two series say “Brazilian coffee is going straight to end users, not to the exchange.” As roasters rebuild inventories deferred for over a year, good lots are absorbed through direct trade, while the coffee tendered to the exchange carries higher rejection risk. That prolongs a two-tier structure in which visible (trade) inventories grow while exchange inventories grow slowly.

5. Robusta — Stocks at a 10-Month High, Nearby Premium Compressing Fast

London November robusta went from $3,396 the prior Friday to $3,337 Mon (-59) → $3,260 Tue (-77) → $3,317 Wed (intraday $3,218, a 3.5-month low) → $3,290 Thu (-27) → about $3,367 Fri (+77, +2.34%), a weekly change of about -$29 (-0.85%) — similar to arabica’s -0.68%.

Structurally, robusta is weaker. ICE robusta certified stocks rose to 5,398 lots (a 10-month high), up 3,020 tonnes over Sep 1–23 per StoneX. Nearby spreads swung sharply: Nov/Jan went $25 → $11 (Tue) → $18 (Thu), Nov/Mar $33 → $16 → $30. On supply, Vaisala said abundant rain has improved soil moisture in Vietnam’s Central Highlands, and new-crop Vietnamese coffee arrives from late October–November. With Brazil’s conilon crop down 6.6% per Conab, robusta’s weakness rests on Vietnam and exchange-stock factors, not Brazil.

The New York–London arbitrage (Dec arabica minus Nov robusta) widened slightly from about 124.4¢ on Tuesday to about 126.1¢/lb on Thursday — still historically very wide.

Stock divergence update: Last week we judged that the ‘arabica low vs robusta high’ stock gap had entered a normalisation phase. This week normalisation happened on only one side — robusta stocks kept rising (10-month high) while arabica stalled. The exchange supply gap between the two varieties widened again. For roasters considering blend adjustments, robusta sourcing conditions are relatively more favourable.

6. Positioning and Technicals — First Signs of Oversold Relief

December fell -65.75¢ (-19.6%) from 335.50¢ on Aug 25 to Wednesday’s 269.75¢ intraday low, then rebounded +8.85¢ (+3.3%) from that low to Friday’s 278.60¢. Both the Wednesday and Friday rallies were attributed to ‘oversold conditions + weaker dollar → fund short covering’ (Safras, Barchart). Brazilian commentary (citing Notícias Agrícolas) said speculators had rebuilt a net short. With shorts in place, any weather catalyst could produce outsized rebounds — that is what changed this week.

Technically, CocoaIntel’s Thursday-based levels are resistance at 278.97 / 282.58 / 286.07¢ and support at 271.87 / 268.38 / 264.77¢. Friday’s 278.60¢ settle sits just below first resistance; reclaiming and holding 280¢ is the first condition for a confirmed rebound. Wednesday’s 269.75¢ low, overlapping the 268¢ support area, is now a double-bottom candidate.

※ CFTC COT as of Sep 22: the single aggregator figure (IndexBox, managed money 33,136 long / 17,330 short) conflicts with Brazilian reports of a renewed speculative net short, so we withhold the number.

7. FX and Macro — A Weaker Real Supports Exports, a Weaker Dollar Supports Price

USD/BRL rose from the 5.14 area the prior Friday (Trading Economics 5.1419; Cal’s 5.1450); the real hit a 3.5-week low on Thursday (Barchart) and ended Friday at about 5.19 (-0.06%, NC News). A weaker real raises Brazilian producers’ incentive to sell in dollar terms, a headwind for futures. On Friday, however, a falling dollar index (DXY) triggered short covering across softs, including coffee.

Implication: Coffee this week was a tug-of-war between a weaker real (bearish) and a weaker global dollar (bullish). In local terms, December at 278.60¢ × 132.276 lb × 5.19 equals about R$1,913/bag, virtually unchanged from last week’s R$1,909 (Cal’s). The weaker real exactly offset the lower dollar price, which helps explain why producer selling did not surge (StoneX: “limited commercial activity”). BRL volatility around Brazil’s first-round presidential election (Oct 4) is a variable for the coming week.

Implications

1. Separate ‘total supply’ from ‘deliverable supply’. Brazil’s crop is a record, but exchange-grade coffee remains scarce. Certified stocks are ~40% of a year ago, and this week’s rebuild stalled at grading. Price weakness is likely to show up mainly as wider discounts on low-quality, off-grade coffee, while good, certifiable lots fall less — a process of quality differentiation.

2. The marginal impact of bearish news is fading. A record Conab, shipments +39% and robusta stocks at a 10-month high all arrived in one week, yet the weekly loss was just -0.68%. A four-week, ~17% (settlement) correction has discounted much of the supply story. Further downside needs ‘new’ bearish news — e.g. confirmation of good fruit set after the heat, or a genuine resumption of certified-stock growth.

3. The risk asymmetry is starting to tilt upward. Bearish drivers (crop size) are now known; bullish drivers (2027 weather, El Niño, quality) are not. With speculators back to net short, if flowering-season heat turns into real damage, short covering could lift prices quickly. That both the Wednesday and Friday rallies were short covering shows the fuel exists.

4. For buyers: a scale-in zone, but tighten quality specs. December sits in a 270–280¢ box, about 17% below the peak. Balancing remaining downside (264–268¢ support) against upside risk (heat, El Niño), it is more sensible to fix part of Q4 2026–Q1 2027 needs in stages and hedge the rest against upside (e.g. with options) than to fix everything at once. For Brazilian origin, cup-quality risk in lots with high Fusarium or ground-swept content argues for tighter specifications and sample approval.

5. Robusta is the more comfortable market. Rising robusta stocks, compressing nearby spreads and imminent Vietnamese new crop make sourcing conditions friendlier than in arabica. With the arabica–robusta arb still around 126¢/lb, raising robusta’s share in blends offers meaningful savings. That said, Brazil’s smaller conilon crop (-6.6%) means less buffer if Vietnamese supply disappoints.

Market Outlook & Risks

Short-Term Market Structure (1–3 Months)

Near term, December is likely to search for direction within a 268–286¢ range. The floor is framed by Wednesday’s low (269.75¢) and CocoaIntel’s second support (268.38¢); the ceiling by third resistance (286.07¢) and last Monday’s high area (283.50¢). The deciding variables, in order: (1) fruit-set field reports after the Sep 26–30 heat (early October), (2) whether ICE certified stocks resume net growth and grading pass rates, (3) the pace of October Cecáfé shipments, and (4) the real around the Oct 4 election.

Base case (~55%): sideways in a 270–285¢ box. Heat damage is confirmed locally but not broadly, certified stocks edge higher, and Brazilian shipments stay strong. Price oscillates between short covering and supply pressure.
Upside case (~25%): rebound above 290¢. Dryness persists after the heat, reports of poor fruit set and flower scorch (estrelinha) spread, grading rejections continue and nearby backwardation re-widens, accompanied by rapid liquidation of speculative shorts.
Downside case (~20%): drop below 265¢. Regular early-October rain confirms good fruit set, certified stocks top 260k bags and resume net growth, and a weaker real stimulates producer selling.

Medium-Term Outlook (3–12 Months)

Over the medium term the global balance is clearly turning to surplus — ICO 3.0m bags in 2025/26, StoneX 10m bags in 2026, and USDA world 2026/27 output of a record 189.7m bags. Trading Economics’ model points to about 241.7¢ in 12 months. But Brazil’s 2027 crop carries three simultaneous uncertainties: an off-year, a strong El Niño, and early/multiple flowerings. If the early bloom develops well, an early May 2027 harvest would shorten the supply gap (bearish); if fruit set disappoints, the entire 2027/28 supply outlook will be reset. The medium-term trend is lower, but path volatility should stay high.

Key Risk Factors

  • Upside — flowering heat and dryness: INMET’s red alert (Sep 26–30) overlaps core South Minas areas. Confirmed fruit-set failure in the early bloom would force 2027 crop estimates lower.
  • Upside — grading bottleneck: If Fusarium and ground-swept coffee keep rejection rates high, deliverable exchange supply stays constrained.
  • Upside — short covering: A renewed speculative net short (per Brazilian reports) is fuel for sharp rallies on any weather event.
  • Upside — disease: A wet winter and hail wounds have opened more entry points for pathogens on new growth.
  • Downside — export pipeline: 2.97m bags of certificates of origin signal heavy late-September/October shipments.
  • Downside — robusta supply: Vietnamese new crop (from late October) and robusta stocks at a 10-month high.
  • Downside — weaker real: Further BRL weakness around the Oct 4 election could boost producer selling.
  • Downside — early harvest: A well-developed early bloom could bring a May 2027 harvest, shortening the supply gap.

Key Issues This Week

  • Certified-stock rebuild stalls: ~30k pending bags processed but net additions near zero — over half of Brazilian coffee rejected at grading.
  • Conab record 67.6m bags: All growth in arabica (+34.8%). Market treated it as confirmation (-0.20%).
  • Rebound from a 3-month low: 269.75¢ intraday (-19.6% from peak), then short covering to 278.60¢; weekly loss narrowed to -0.68%.
  • Flowering heat alert: Red alert for South Minas Sep 26–30 — the first real weather test for the 2027 crop.

What to Watch Next Week

  • Heat-impact field reports (after Sep 30): fruit set in South Minas and the Cerrado, flower scorch (estrelinha), and rainfall after the cold front passes.
  • Somar weekly rainfall (Mondays Sep 28, Oct 5): below-normal Minas rain in the week to Sep 27, combined with heat, would be bullish.
  • ICE certified stocks: whether they regain 260k bags, and grading pass rates. Improving pass rates = bearish; continued rejections = bullish.
  • Cecáfé final September exports (early October): how fast the 2.97m bags of certificates convert into shipments.
  • CFTC COT (released Oct 2, as of Sep 29): size of the speculative net short — the fuel for short covering.
  • BRL around the Oct 4 first-round election: a break above 5.20 could increase producer selling.
  • Technical levels: December reclaiming and holding 280¢ (upside); holding 268¢ support (downside).

Overall Assessment

This week the coffee market shifted its centre of gravity from a question of quantity to questions of quality and weather. Conab’s record 67.6m-bag estimate removed the last uncertainty over the size of Brazil’s 2026 supply, and the market absorbed it calmly (-0.20%). Two new questions emerged instead. First, how much of that record crop is exchange grade? The stalling of the certified-stock rebuild at grading exposed a quality bottleneck created by Fusarium and ground-swept coffee. Second, can the 2027 crop withstand the heat? A red heat-wave alert arrived right after a bloom that came a month early.

Prices fell for a fourth straight week, but the pace slowed markedly (weekly -1.82% → -0.68%; close-to-close range 14.00¢ → 6.30¢). Short covering from Wednesday’s 269.75¢ 3-month low shows the diminishing marginal impact of bearish news. Our base case is range-bound direction-finding within 270–285¢, with the next move set by early-October fruit-set reports and grading pass rates. The medium-term surplus picture holds, but current prices embed almost no 2027 weather risk. For buyers, rather than deferring everything in hope of lower prices, combining staged fixing near the bottom of the range with upside hedges is the more risk-efficient choice.
