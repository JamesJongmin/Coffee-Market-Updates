Weekly Market Summary

The narrative flipped again — from last week's "rain-driven harvest delay" back to "dry-weather harvest acceleration and a record crop." September arabica was pressured to a one-month low (front month in the low-310s cents) mid-week by dry, warm Brazil weather (Somar: no rain in Minas Gerais in the week to Aug 2), the USDA's record 2026/27 world-crop outlook (189.7M bags), and gradual producer selling.

But late in the week, the macro backdrop drove a reversal. With the dollar index at a 7-week low on Friday and the Brazilian real firmer (Fed on hold, wide rate differential), producer selling slowed and dollar-priced coffee grew more attractive; KCU26 surged +13.90c (+4.32%) to a 1-week high (≈335.6c). A further drop in ICE arabica certified stocks to a 2.5-year low (244,172 bags) reinforced the upside.

The net result was a modest ~+1% weekly gain versus the prior Friday (332.10c), but the intraweek range was wide. Downside (weather, record crop) and upside (macro, low stocks) took turns dominating within a single week — reaffirming a regime in which coffee is driven more by macro (dollar/real) and inventories than by pure supply fundamentals.

Key dynamic of the week: Mid-week was dominated by the dry-weather/record-crop downside; Friday by the weaker dollar/firmer real/low-stocks upside. Pure supply/demand (a record crop) points down, but 2.5-year-low stocks and macro swings offset it, repeating a pattern of macro- and inventory-led rebounds in thin liquidity. Friday's bounce looks more like an FX/short-covering move than a fundamental improvement.

Fundamental Analysis

1. Weather & Harvest — the dry turn drove the downside

Last week's price-supportive rain gave way to dry, warm conditions this week. Per Somar Meteorologia, Minas Gerais saw no rain in the week to Aug 2, accelerating harvesting and drying — a bearish factor that speeds new-crop flows to market. Still, the harvest lags last year: Cooxupé 67.3% as of 7/31 (74.2% a year ago) and Safras 64% as of 7/15 (77% a year ago). Quality concerns from June–July rains also linger.

Implication: Dryness speeds volume flows, but the harvest still trails last year and quality risk for high-grade washed arabica remains. As long as the gap between "record crop (quantity)" and "certified-grade spot (quality)" persists, dry-weather dips stay floored by low stocks.

ICE arabica certified stocks stood at 244,172 bags on Friday (Trading Economics reported ~251,417 as of 8/7), a 2.5-year low and approx -68% below a year ago (~754,516 bags). Until new-crop coffee enters warehouses at certified grade, spot tightness persists — the floor and catalyst for Friday's rebound. By contrast, robusta stocks rose to a 4.5-month high (4,261 lots), so arabica and robusta inventories diverged.

3. Balance — USDA record crop vs low stocks

The medium-term picture is still record supply. On July 22, the USDA projected 2026/27 world output up +6.0% (+10.8M bags) to a record 189.7M bags (world arabica +12%, robusta -0.7%; world ending stocks +1.9M to 26.3M). The FAS (June 3) put Brazil's crop at 71.9M bags (+14% y/y, a record). Flows are strong too: Vietnam Jan–Jul exports +21.1% to 1.31 MMT, 2025/26 output 1.76 MMT (29.4M bags). This record-crop consensus anchors the medium-term downside bias.

4. El Niño — 2026/27 flowering risk as asymmetric upside

On July 8, the US Climate Prediction Center (CPC) warned that this year's El Niño could be one of the strongest in 75+ years. The market's focus is Brazil's September–October flowering (2026/27 crop), when disrupted rains would raise fruit-set risk (some traders flag possible Sep–Oct rain delays). Flood, drought and temperature risks across Asia and South America also loom through the second half. This residual upside raises the value of low-cost call-option hedges against the record-crop consensus.

5. Macro & Positioning — a weaker dollar and firmer real led

USD/BRL: the real firmed from a 3-week low (~5.145 on 8/4) to ~5.08 on 8/7, while the dollar index sank to a 7-week low (99.53). Despite the Fed's hold (3.50–3.75%) and the BCB's expected 25bp Selic cut (to 14.0%), a wide rate differential and strong Brazilian employment supported the real, slowing producer selling and directly aiding Friday's coffee upside. Positioning: Friday's surge looks largely like macro-led short-covering, though new CFTC net positioning could not be independently cross-confirmed this week and specific figures are excluded.

Implications

1. Macro back in the driver's seat — FX/stocks > weather: Friday's surge came from a weaker dollar, firmer real and low stocks, not improved supply/demand. It reaffirms a regime with higher sensitivity to macro and inventories than to pure fundamentals. For roasters and traders, FX-driven procurement-cost swings are the key near-term variable.

2. Low stocks pin the floor: ICE arabica at ~244K bags (2.5-year low, -68% y/y) is a structural floor that keeps buyers stepping into dry-weather and record-crop dips. Until new-crop coffee accumulates at certified grade, the "record crop yet spot-tight" paradox keeps generating rebound rallies.

3. Rebound is short-covering/FX-led → reversal risk: Friday's gain is closer to macro/positioning than fundamentals. If the dollar bounces or the real weakens again, producer selling revives and a quick reversal is possible. Two-way gap risk is elevated in thin liquidity.

4. El Niño/quality = residual asymmetric upside: A 75-year-strong El Niño's Sep–Oct flowering risk (2026/27) and June–July rain quality concerns raise the value of low-cost upside hedges against the record-crop consensus. Robusta, by contrast, is capped by surging Vietnam exports and rising stocks.

Market Outlook & Risks

Near-Term Structure (1–3 months)

The trend is a high-volatility, macro- and inventory-led range. Support sits at 320c and 310c (mid-week lows); resistance at 335c (weekly high), 345c and 363–370c (the 2025 supply zone). The dollar/real direction is the near-term swing factor: continued dollar weakness/real strength tests the upper band, a dollar bounce brings a pullback. With low stocks flooring dips and the record crop/Vietnam exports capping rallies, we expect a high-vol, neutral-to-bullish range.

Technical view: From a mid-week 1-month low (low-310s) to a Friday +4.32% recovery to a 1-week high (≈335.6c). Support 320/310c; resistance 335/345c and 363–370c. Two-way gaps are possible on FX, inventory and El Niño headlines in thin liquidity. Short-term momentum favors the rebound, but its macro dependence means durability needs confirmation.

Medium-Term Outlook (3–12 months)

Medium-term fundamentals remain record supply (world 189.7M, Brazil 71.9M, Vietnam +21.1%), a downside bias. But (1) 2.5-year-low certified stocks (-68% y/y), (2) a 75-year-strong El Niño's 2026/27 flowering risk, and (3) June–July rain quality concerns act as buffers/wildcards, making "structural downward pressure + intermittent macro/inventory-led spikes" the base case. Whether new-crop coffee accumulates at certified grade and relieves the low-stock condition is the directional watershed.

Key Risk Factors

  • Dollar/Real (two-way): continued dollar weakness/real strength is bullish; a dollar bounce/weaker real brings producer-selling downside. Direction can flip within a week.
  • ICE stocks (bullish/buffer): 244,172 bags (2.5-year low, -68% y/y). Spot tightness curbs selloffs and catalyzes rebounds.
  • Brazil weather (two-way): continued dryness accelerates the harvest (bearish); disrupted flowering-season rains raise 2026/27 risk (bullish).
  • El Niño (2026/27, bullish): a 75-year-strong event — disrupted Sep–Oct flowering would be asymmetrically bullish.
  • Structural supply (bearish): world 189.7M, Brazil 71.9M, Vietnam +21.1% — a sustained medium-term drag.
  • Robusta (bearish): surging Vietnam exports and 4.5-month-high stocks cap robusta and widen the arabica–robusta spread.

Overall Analysis

This was a week in which a dry-weather mid-week slide was reversed by a Friday macro-driven surge. September arabica fell to a one-month low (low-310s) on dry weather, harvest acceleration and the USDA record crop, then jumped +4.32% (+13.90c) on Friday — aided by a 7-week-low dollar index, a firmer real and 2.5-year-low ICE stocks — to a 1-week high (≈335.6c), leaving the week up roughly +1%. Downside (supply) and upside (macro/stocks) took turns within a single week.

The most important shift is that the driver moved to "macro and inventories." Even aware of the record-crop consensus, the market reacted more to the dollar/real and low stocks. Yet the rebound is FX/short-covering in character, so a dollar bounce or weaker real raises reversal risk. As long as the record crop's quantity-quality gap and the 2.5-year-low stocks persist, the big picture is likely a high-vol range plus intermittent macro/inventory-led spikes.

Trading takeaways: a near-term 310–345c high-vol range, with 320/310c support and 335/345c and 363–370c resistance as the pivots. Key monitors: (1) USD/BRL and the dollar index; (2) ICE stocks and grading pipeline; (3) Minas weather, harvest pace and quality; (4) CPC El Niño updates; (5) Vietnam exports and robusta stocks; (6) new COT (when confirmed).

For roasters and importers, it is reasonable to avoid chasing macro-driven spikes, to manage average cost by scaling in on pullbacks, and to run low-cost call-option hedges against El Niño/quality risk. Guard against one-way short bets until the record crop is confirmed in certified stocks.
