Weekly Market Summary

This week's narrative flipped completely, from "two straight down weeks" to a "weather-driven surge." September arabica jumped +1,075 pts (+3.4%) Monday to 324.55c and +4.6% Tuesday, hitting an intraday ~345c (a ~5.5-month high) in two sessions. Last week's bearish story of accelerating harvest, a record crop and tariff exemptions was replaced by rain-delayed Brazil harvest, quality concerns, El Nino risk and ICE certified stocks breaking below 300,000 bags.

The surge came with a pullback. On Wednesday (7/29) a weaker real (a 2.5-week low vs the dollar) spurred Brazilian producer selling and a -1,360 pts (-4.01%) drop to 325.80c, with profit-taking and long liquidation continuing later in the week. Daily volatility was extreme (Mon +3.4%, Tue +4.6%, Wed -4.0%), but the big picture is an estimated weekly gain of roughly +4-5% versus the prior Friday (~313.8c), reversing two down weeks.

The fundamental axes were weather and low stocks. Somar, Cepea and Cooxupé warned that above-average rainfall across Minas Gerais and the coffee belt was slowing harvest and threatening bean quality, while ICE arabica certified stocks fell to 274,168 bags, below 300,000 (lowest since February 2024, approx -65% y/y). Layered on top, NOAA's 67% "Super El Nino" warning highlighted 2027/28 flowering risk and helped trigger fund short-covering.

Key dynamic this week: where last week was "tariff exemption plus accelerating harvest = renewed decline," this week was "rain-delayed harvest, quality worries, low stocks and El Nino = a sharp rebound." Upside catalysts (weather, stocks, El Nino) clashed with downside buffers (real-driven producer selling, the record-crop consensus) within the same week, producing near-record volatility. The rally looks heavily short-covering driven, so a reversal in weather headlines carries meaningful pullback risk.

Fundamental Analysis

1. Weather & Harvest - Rain Delays Drive the Upside

The real driver of this week's rebound was above-average rainfall across Brazil's coffee belt. Per Somar and Cepea, rain in Minas Gerais and other key regions delayed harvesting, drying and sorting and threatened bean quality, especially high-grade washed arabica. Cooxupe's harvest reading was 58.3% as of July 24 (from 47.3% a week earlier), lagging last year's 67.1%. Drier weather late in the week accelerated fieldwork again, but the delay/quality premium persists. Frost risk was ruled out this week (temperatures above 10C).

Implication: a "record crop" remains the consensus, but rain-driven quality loss and harvest delays separate the crop's volume from its quality. Even with ample volume, spot tightness in certifiable washed arabica can persist, and combined with low stocks it fuels repeated upside snapbacks.

2. El Nino - 2027/28 Flowering Risk Is an Asymmetric Upside

NOAA put the odds of a "Super El Nino" at 67%, warning it could be the strongest on record (some discussion cites the strongest in 150 years); Japan's Meteorological Agency confirmed El Nino had formed in June. The market's focus is Brazil's September-October flowering (the 2027/28 crop), where disrupted rainfall would raise fruit-set risk. A Cooxupe forum also flagged premature 2027/28 flowering and flower/fruit abortion risk from heavy rain and heat. Risks outside Brazil (Central America, Asia) are seen as larger (Hedgepoint). The Peterson Institute estimated a Super El Nino could cause about $686bn in global losses in year one.

ICE arabica certified stocks fell to 274,168 bags on July 29, below 300,000 and the lowest since February 2024, down about -65% from roughly 792,000 bags a year earlier. Inventory is concentrated in Honduras (approx 89,892 bags) and Uganda (approx 39,014 bags), with Brazil-origin volume still scarce. Until the new crop is graded and enters warehouses, spot tightness persists - a floor and a catalyst for this week's rebound.

4. Exports & Balance - Record Crop vs New-Crop Flows

The medium-term picture is still one of record supply. USDA FAS sees Brazil 2026/27 at 71.9 million bags (a record), with global production, exports and consumption each hitting all-time highs (+6%, +9%, +3.6%). Rabobank raised its global arabica surplus estimate to 9.5 million bags. Volumes are flowing: Brazil's June green coffee exports were 2.64 million bags (+14.4% y/y, Cecafe), and Vietnam's H1 exports 1.1 million MT (+9.7% y/y). But Vietnamese and Indonesian robusta harvests are winding down, thinning spot supply and lifting Indonesia premiums from $80 to $160/ton.

5. Macro & Positioning - Weaker Real, Short-Covering, Geopolitics

USD/BRL: the real weakened to a 2.5-week low against the dollar mid-week, spurring Brazilian producer selling and triggering Wednesday/Friday profit-taking (precise level is single-source). Positioning: the surge is widely seen as heavily fund and speculative short-covering after last week's decline (Comunicaffe/StoneX). However, we could not independently confirm a fresh CFTC net-position figure for this week from two sources, so a specific number is omitted. Macro: a re-escalation in the Middle East (US strikes on Iran, oil +7%) and a Fed hold (the new chair's first meeting, with three dissents for a hike) framed a risk-off backdrop.

Implications

1. Narrative reversal = weather and stocks retake control: last week's bearish story (tariff exemption, accelerating harvest) flipped to a bullish one (rain, quality, low stocks, El Nino) in a single week. The market's sensitivity to weather and stocks is high enough to temporarily ignore the "record crop / surplus" consensus. For roasters and traders, the risk of a renewed spike in near-term sourcing costs is back.

2. The crop's volume and quality are diverging: the record crop (Brazil 71.9M, global surplus 9.5M) remains intact, but rain-driven quality loss and certified stocks below 300K spotlight the reality of spot tightness in high-grade washed coffee. Until this volume-quality gap narrows, downside is limited and snapback rallies can recur.

3. The rebound is short-covering and weather-premium led: this surge is closer to short-covering and a weather risk premium than to improving fundamentals. With real-driven producer selling capping the top, a calming of weather headlines could bring a quick reversal. Near-record daily volatility raises two-way gap risk.

4. El Nino = residual asymmetric upside: NOAA's "67% Super El Nino" raises upside risk to September-October flowering (2027/28) and to Central America/Asia crops. Against a bumper-crop/surplus consensus, the value of low-cost call-option hedges has, if anything, improved.

Market Outlook & Risks

Near-Term Structure (1-3 Months)

The trend is a weather-driven, high-volatility test of range highs. Support sits at 320c and 310c (prior low zone); resistance at 340c, 345c (weekly high) and 363-370c (2025 supply zone). Rain, El Nino and low stocks push the top up while real weakness and the record crop cap it - a high-volatility, upside-biased range. Position unwinds ahead of the August index roll and September options expiry add volatility.

Technical view: after a Tuesday intraday high near ~345c (a ~5.5-month peak), Wednesday retraced to 325.80c. Support 320/310c, resistance 340/345c and 363-370c. In thin liquidity, weather/El Nino headlines can produce two-way gaps. RSI is cooling from overbought, so near-term corrections and renewed advances can alternate.

Medium-Term Outlook (3-12 Months)

Medium-term fundamentals still tilt bearish on record supply (Brazil 71.9M, global surplus 9.5M). But (1) rain-driven quality loss and certified stocks below 300K, (2) NOAA's 67% Super El Nino risk to 2027/28 flowering, and (3) short-covering and positioning rolls act as buffers and wildcards, making "structural downward pressure plus intermittent weather-driven spikes" the base case. Whether the new crop is graded and rebuilds warehouse stocks is the key swing factor.

Key Risk Factors

  • Brazil weather (two-way): continued rain delays harvest and hurts quality (bullish); a return to dry weather accelerates it (bearish). Direction reversed within the week.
  • El Nino (2027/28, upside): NOAA 67% Super El Nino - asymmetric upside if Sep-Oct flowering is disrupted.
  • ICE stocks (upside/buffer): 274,168 bags (below 300K, approx -65% y/y). Spot tightness caps declines and fuels rebounds.
  • Structural supply (downside): Brazil 71.9M, global surplus 9.5M, Vietnam +9.7% - keeps medium-term pressure.
  • Real & macro (downside/wildcard): real-driven producer selling; Middle East conflict and Fed risk-off.
  • Positioning (two-way): short-covering-led rebound - volatility around the August index roll and September options expiry.

Comprehensive Analysis

The week's story was a weather-driven rebound that reversed two straight down weeks. September arabica surged +3.4% and +4.6% on Monday and Tuesday to an intraday ~345c (a ~5.5-month high), retraced -4.0% (325.80c) on Wednesday's weaker real, and is estimated to have closed the week up roughly +4-5%. Upside catalysts - rain-delayed harvest, quality worries, El Nino and certified stocks below 300K - clashed with downside buffers of a record crop and a weaker real.

The most important shift is that control passed back to "weather and stocks." The market temporarily ignored the "record crop / surplus" consensus to trade pure weather risk. But the rebound is heavily short-covering and risk-premium driven, so a calming of weather headlines carries reversal risk. As long as the volume-quality gap and low stocks persist, the big picture is likely a high-volatility range with intermittent weather-driven spikes.

Trading takeaway: a near-term high-volatility 310-345c range, with 320/310c support and 340/345c and 363-370c resistance as the pivots. Key monitors: (1) Minas rainfall, harvest pace and quality; (2) ICE stocks and grading queue; (3) NOAA El Nino updates; (4) USD/BRL and producer selling; (5) August index roll and September options expiry; (6) fresh COT (once confirmed).

For roasters and importers, it is prudent to avoid chasing weather-driven spikes, scale into pullbacks to manage average cost, and pair that with low-cost call-option hedges against El Nino and quality risk. Until the record crop is confirmed in certified stocks, one-directional bearish bets warrant caution.
